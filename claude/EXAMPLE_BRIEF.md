# Example brief (baseline from the data only)

This is what the brief looks like when only the project data is used and no outside signals have been collected. It shows the shape, not real news.

**North Adriatic offtake radar, baseline**

1. **Headline.** In the core area, 61 installations could use about 276 kt of hydrogen per year if the assumed sector shares hold, and the top five installations account for roughly 55% of the potential. Refining and power plants dominate; steel and paper follow.
2. **New signals.** None collected yet. The weekly search would fill this section, each item with a link and marked confirmed or unverified.
3. **Buyers to watch** (from the data, ranked by estimated hydrogen potential):

| Installation | Sector | CO2 2025 (kt) | H2 potential (kt/yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| INA refinery, Rijeka | Refining | 916 | 68.2 |
| Termoelektrarna Šoštanj | Power and heat | 1,803 | 40.3 |
| Acciaierie Bertoli Safau | Steel and metals | 236 | 15.9 |
| Duino plant | Paper and pulp | 113 | 13.5 |
| TE Plomin 2 | Power and heat | 591 | 13.2 |

   Power and heat uses a low share (0.15) because fuel switching to hydrogen competes with electrification, so those figures are the most uncertain.
4. **Number of the week.** Break-even delivered hydrogen price at gas 35 EUR/MWh and ETS 80 EUR/t: 33.3 x 35/1000 + 6.7 x 80/1000 = about 1.70 EUR/kg. Compare this with the current delivered price of green hydrogen to see the gap.
5. **Open questions.** Which of the top five has a decarbonisation plan we can verify? Is the synthetic fuel licence a process technology or production rights? Which assumptions should an expert recalibrate first?
