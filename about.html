What this is, and why we propose it

A first step towards a buyer-first intelligence service for hydrogen and e-fuel projects in the North Adriatic.

What this is

A small prototype built only from public data. It lists the industrial installations in Slovenia, Croatia and Friuli Venezia Giulia that already report CO₂ under the EU ETS, estimates how much hydrogen each could use, and adds a layer for synthetic aviation fuel (e-SAF) based on regional airport traffic. Everything is traceable to a public source and every assumption is shown on the page.

It is a sketch, not a finished product. The sector shares and fuel figures are assumptions that need calibration with industry experts, and the page has no live signals yet.

Why start from buyers, not investors

Investors follow offtake

A hydrogen or e-fuel plant is financed when someone credibly agrees to buy its output. A clear, ranked list of likely buyers is therefore the strongest starting point for an investor conversation, and it also tells you which investors would care.

A list of investors is not enough

Lists of decision-makers and press releases are visible to everyone and say little about timing. What matters is who needs the product, how much, and at what price it starts to work.

The price claim needs evidence

Any pitch built on a future price parity will be asked what has to be true for it. A live model with the gas price, the carbon price and the delivered hydrogen price as inputs answers this, and it can double as the data room.

Relationships plus data

Strong personal networks already open doors. Data shows which doors are worth opening and why now, so existing relationships are used on the right targets.

What a service could look like

  • Demand map of likely buyers, kept up to date as new emissions and permit data are published.
  • Trigger signals: events that change a buyer's position, such as grant and auction results, cancelled projects (whose buyers are left without a supplier), permits, tenders, strategy changes.
  • Warm paths: with people's consent, who in the network knows a decision-maker at a target company.
  • Price model for the "what must be true" question, with scenarios for gas, carbon, electrolyser cost and logistics.
  • One page a week and alerts when a trigger fires, checked by a person for reliability before it goes out. It is a service run by us, so nobody on the receiving side has to operate anything.

Why fuel comes first

EU rules require fuel suppliers to blend growing shares of synthetic aviation fuel, which creates a regulated demand for hydrogen and captured CO₂. That makes it the clearest buyer story, so the fuel pages lead. The Europe e-SAF map shows the continent-wide supply side, and the Adriatic page covers the regional demand. The hydrogen page covers industrial users, and industrial CO₂ sources double as candidate CO₂ suppliers for e-fuel plants. The fuel market is EU-wide, so regional airport figures show the local share, not the whole opportunity.

Limits to keep in mind

  • Plant locations come from the account holder's postcode with name-based corrections, so some rows need checking.
  • The EU ETS counts fossil CO₂ only, so biogenic CO₂ is not visible.
  • Maritime fuels and small installations outside the ETS are not covered.
  • Assumptions are unvalidated. Treat the numbers as an order of magnitude.

Proposed next steps

  1. Calibrate the sector shares with an industry expert and fix plant locations.
  2. Wire two or three signal sources and run them for a month.
  3. Build the price model with the project's own cost assumptions.
  4. Decide together whether the weekly brief is worth continuing.
